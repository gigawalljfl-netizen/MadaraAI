Preamble

This Privacy Policy and Terms of Service (the “Policy”) explains how Giga Wall JFL (the “Developer,” “we,” or “us”) handles information and personal data when you use the M.A.D.A.R.A.AI Android application, short for “Mobile Advanced Digital Assistant for Runtime” (the “App”). The App’s assistant persona is named Madara (the “Assistant”). It describes collection, processing, storage, use, sharing, and protection of information.

This version explains the App’s expanded capabilities: programming and project-progress tracking; automatic conversation highlights with a target of retaining about 90% of meaningful, notable details (not full conversation transcripts); user-created routines stored on the device with scheduled triggers and checkpoint-based recovery; silent mode with a wake word; SMS, calls, and contacts; time, location, and weather; optional capture of audio played by the device; Termux integration; web search; music playback; autonomous text conversations; image-file analysis on request; a lightweight on-device work indicator; support for Portuguese, English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Russian, and Korean; and guidance for building Android apps with GitHub Actions. Each capability and its data flow is described below.

  • 4.1. The App is an AI assistant designed to help with productivity, automation, programming, and tasks on your device.
  • 4.2. The App is designed to minimize data, preferring processing and storage on your device rather than on external servers.
  • 4.3. Local processing keeps ordinary device commands and interactions on the device, except for content sent to Google or another Third-Party Service when needed for a requested feature, as described in Sections 3 and 9.

Depending on your device and speech-recognition provider, Android itself may process the audio locally or through a Google service; the Developer does not control that system behavior. You can turn the wake word off in Settings; in that case, resume by tapping the screen or the notification.

  • 5.4.1. Permissions: READ_SMS (read existing messages), RECEIVE_SMS (detect a new message), and SEND_SMS (send messages).

Sending: the App sends an SMS only at your direction, to a recipient and with text you specify. “Send SMS directly” in Settings controls whether it sends without opening the messaging app for review.   • 5.4.5. Storage: while the App is running, it may keep a small number of recent received messages in volatile memory so you can say “reply to the last SMS.” Messages in Android’s messaging app remain part of Android’s local storage and are not copied to a Developer server.

  • 5.5.1. Permissions: READ_CONTACTS (find a number by name) and CALL_PHONE (start a call).
  • 5.5.2. The App searches contacts or starts a call only when you ask. “Call directly when I ask” in Settings controls whether the call starts immediately or the Android dialer opens with the number ready for your confirmation. You can turn this option off at any time.

  • 5.6.1. Permissions: ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION and ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION.
  • 5.6.2. Purpose: answer requests such as “where am I?”, “what time is it?” with the correct time zone, and “what is the weather?” without requiring you to name a city.
  • 5.6.3. Location comes from the device (GPS and/or network) and may be used to describe a place through reverse geocoding or to query Open-Meteo with coordinates (Section 9). The App briefly caches the last position in memory to avoid repeatedly requesting GPS. The Developer does not receive your location.
  • 5.6.4. Transparency about accuracy: a position based on mobile towers or Wi-Fi can be wrong by hundreds of meters. The App records and reports the estimated accuracy in meters and its source (satellite GPS or network). When the margin is large, Madara is instructed to call the position approximate rather than claim an exact address. You can request precise mode, which waits for GPS to improve the result.
  • 5.6.5. Without these permissions, the device time zone still supplies the time, and weather can be checked when you give a city.

Technical limits: capture is limited to 30 seconds at a time, only for apps Android permits (such as media, games, or unspecified audio), and never captures the microphone through this feature. While capture is active, microphone audio sent to Google is paused so the two sources are not mixed.

    • 5.23. Saved Routines and Local Schedules

  • 5.23.1. At your request, the App can store a named routine with up to ten ordered steps. A routine, its schedule, and per-step progress/outcomes are saved only in internal App storage on this device and excluded from Android cloud backups and device transfers. The App does not sync them to Firebase or send the stored routine file to the Developer. When you ask the AI to create, manage, or run a routine in a conversation, the associated request and tool-call data are sent to Google under Section 3. For safe recovery, routine steps are limited to repeatable, direct device settings: setting media volume or screen brightness, enabling/disabling auto-rotation, turning the flashlight on/off, and choosing one of the App’s visual themes.   • 5.23.3. You must explicitly request the schedule. A routine can run once at a local date/time, every day, or on selected weekdays. Android’s inexact alarm service may delay a scheduled run to conserve battery; the App does not promise execution at an exact minute. Schedules are rebuilt after reboot, app updates, and device-time or time-zone changes. You can cancel future scheduled runs without deleting the routine.
  • 5.23.4. The App records the completed-step checkpoint and each step’s latest outcome locally. If execution is interrupted, it resumes at the current step. Because only explicit “set” actions are allowed, safely reapplying the current step does not toggle or duplicate a completed operation. Transient failures may be retried automatically up to three times with increasing delays; Android’s battery rules may defer those retries. Permission failures stop the run and are reported. You can inspect the routine’s status and resume it manually.
  • 5.23.5. An explicitly scheduled routine, or a bounded retry/recovery belonging to a run you already requested, may continue while the App is closed. No unrelated device action is started autonomously by this feature.

6. AI Memory — What Is Stored

  • 6.1. To let Madara remember useful context, the App extracts short conversation highlights on the device, without requiring the model to call a save function for every item. Extraction runs on the user’s speech transcript. The model may also save a fact when appropriate.
  • 6.2. Highlight categories include humor, websites, problems, places, preferences, people, work, plans, projects, and other useful facts.
  • 6.3. The retention design target is to capture about 90% of meaningful, noteworthy details identified by the App and the AI—not 90% of every spoken word, not a full transcript, and not a guarantee of a measured success rate. A more inclusive local confidence threshold and model-assisted saves are used to reduce missed highlights. Duplicate items are filtered. Memory can still miss details or keep an item that is not useful.
  • 6.4. The App does not intentionally save conversation audio, complete transcripts, images, passwords, API keys, authentication tokens, complete payment-card numbers, third-party SMS contents, or items you have asked it to forget. Do not share secrets with the AI. The App cannot guarantee that an AI model will correctly recognize every secret in free-form conversation.
  • 6.5. Memory is stored in the App’s internal device file and, if you have an account, in your Firebase account (Section 8) so it can survive reinstalling. It is not otherwise sent to the Developer.
  • 6.6. You control Memory: ask “what do you remember?”, ask Madara to forget an item or what it knows about a topic, view statistics in Settings > Memory status, or turn off automatic memory in Settings (“Save conversation highlights automatically”).
  • 6.7. Memory consists of short text summaries with a maximum of 60 items per category; repeated items are not duplicated, and the summary included in a model session has a size limit. It is not a growing recording of your life.

7. Autonomous Conversations (With Another AI or by SMS)

  • 7.1. Purpose: after your explicit instruction (for example, “talk with ChatGPT about X until I say stop” or “reply to João by SMS”), the App can continue an exchange without waiting for you to issue each next instruction.
  • 7.2. Another AI: the conversation uses Google’s text API (Section 3.3) with your API key. The App reports the model and configured turn limit.
  • 7.3. SMS: the App provides messages received from the agreed phone number to the conversation and sends replies through SEND_SMS (Section 5.4).
